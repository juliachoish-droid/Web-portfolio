Project Overview

This project began with a simple question:

What if records were not just content to be stored, but spaces I could re-enter and experience again?

I often scatter my travel memories—
photos on one platform, text on another,
and emotions and context somewhere else entirely.
As a result, records continue to accumulate,
but are rarely revisited as lived experiences.

This project proposes a web environment
that gathers fragmented travel records into a
personal space, allowing me to revisit and explore my
memories through spatial interaction, and enabling
those records to be shared naturally when appropriate.

Insight & Shift

Insight

Through personas and user research, I found that users are accustomed to visually driven, scroll-based platforms, but struggle to distinguish marketing-oriented content from authentic personal experiences.

As a result, personal records continue to accumulate, while the context of real experiences fades, making them less likely to be revisited or meaningfully engaged with.

Shift

To address this, I designed a space-based environment where personal records can first be organized and accumulated, rather than immediately shared.

By doing so, I aimed to create a structure in which authentic experiences and personal context remain visible and meaningful, even when those records are shared later.

Problem

Personal travel records are scattered across platforms, causing the context of real experiences to fade over time.

Solution

A space-based web environment where records are first accumulated as a personal space, allowing experiences to remain meaningful and revisitable.

Concept: Records as Space

💡 Concept 1

Rather than organizing records by time or location, this project treats records as a space that users can personally design and arrange.

💡 Concept 2

When records are placed within a space, users are not simply organizing information, but re-entering and experiencing scenes they have created themselves.

💡 Concept 3

In this project, space is not a tool for precise classification, but a device that gives users a reason to return to their memories.

Key Features

Feature 1 — Entry Experience

The homepage is designed as an entry point into a space composed of records. Instead of just showing content, it creates the feeling of stepping into another world. This experience sets the overall tone and atmosphere of the website.

Feature 2 — My Room

Records become objects within a 3D room, which users continuously shape into their own personal space by adding photos, text, and videos over time. Through this process, documentation shifts from simple storage to an interactive, evolving environment.

Feature 3 — Revisit & Share

Records stored in a user’s room are publicly searchable and can be explored by other users. Users can also save selected records created by others to their personal collection for later reference.

Process & Decisions

One of the main challenges of this project was defining a realistic scope and technical direction while designing and implementing the entire website.

In the early stages of development, it was difficult to accurately estimate the required time, technical complexity, and overall feasibility. To address this, I adopted an iterative approach— starting with small, functional demos and gradually expanding the website’s features.

Through this process, I was able to prioritize core functionality and make technical and design decisions grounded in the actual implementation process.

Design Approach

  • UX insights were not treated as assumptions, but were validated and continuously refined through implementation.
  • Small, iterative demos enabled realistic design decisions to be made across the entire project.
  • Research, design, and development were approached as one continuous and coherent process.

Outcome & Reflection

This project goes beyond building a single website. It frames the web as a design medium for structuring and revisiting personal experiences. By designing a format for records themselves, the project explores how individual ways of documenting experiences can be shaped, shared, and revisited through space.
